# CASE FILE
**Question:** Our outdoor-seating cafés seem to sell less and waste more on certain days. Find out why.

## Prime cause
Rainy days (days with precipitation > 5 mm) consistently reduce sales and inflate the waste rate for all three outdoor-seating cafés.  
- **[E3]** shows that for the outdoor-seating stores (CAF-BRU-03, CAF-FIT-01, CAF-STK-02) the sales ratio on rain days versus dry days is 0.69-0.70, i.e. sales drop roughly 30 % when it rains.  
- The same rows report a waste-percentage jump from ~8.7 % (dry) to ~55-56 % on rain days, a > 5-fold increase in waste rate.  
- The effect is specific to rain; stores without outdoor seating (e.g., CAF-BRK-05, CAF-COL-04) have rain-to-dry sales ratios near 0.96 and waste rates barely changing, confirming the outdoor-seating exposure is the driver.

## Suspects
| Factor | Evidence | Verdict |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Public holidays** | **[E4]** - for the outdoor-seating stores (CAF-BRU-03, CAF-FIT-01, CAF-STK-02) the holiday-to-normal sales ratio is ≈1.0 (no significant dip). | Ruled out |
| **Promotions** | **[E5]** - promo days for CAF-HAW-07 (non-outdoor) show sales and waste virtually unchanged versus non-promo days. No outdoor-seating store shows a promo effect in the data. | Ruled out |
| **Weekly waste spikes (E2)** | Several store-product combos show ratios ≥ 2, but these involve products (e.g., Ham & Cheese Croissant at CAF-BRU-03) not uniquely tied to outdoor-seating weather sensitivity. The spikes occur in March, a period without noted rain anomalies in the evidence. | Considered but not the primary driver for the outdoor-seating pattern |
| **Non-rain weekdays** | No evidence of systematic sales decline or waste increase on normal dry days for outdoor-seating locations. | Ruled out |

## Verdict and recommendation
The data point to **rain** as the prime cause of both lower sales and higher waste at the outdoor-seating cafés. On rain days sales fall to roughly 70 % of normal, and the waste rate more than quintuples, likely because unsold perishable items cannot be sold outdoors and are discarded.

**Recommendation:**  
- Implement a rain-day inventory adjustment: reduce preparation of perishable items forecasted for outdoor service when precipitation > 5 mm is predicted.  
- Consider offering indoor-seating incentives or limited-time rain-day specials to capture demand that would otherwise be lost.  
- Monitor daily weather forecasts and align staffing and ordering accordingly to minimise waste while preserving revenue.

),
rb AS (
  SELECT b.*, CAST(w.precipitation_mm AS DOUBLE) AS rain_mm
  FROM base b
  JOIN cases.weather_table w ON b.date = w.date
)
SELECT store_id,
       MAX(outdoor) AS outdoor_seating,
       MAX(area_type) AS area_type,
       SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS rain_rows,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_rain,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_dry,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END)
             / AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS sales_rain_to_dry,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_wasted END)
             / SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS waste_pct_rain,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_wasted END)
             / SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS waste_pct_dry
FROM rb
GROUP BY store_id
ORDER BY sales_rain_to_dry ASC
