# CASE FILE  
**Question:** Our CBD office cafés had some unusually quiet days this year. What explains them?  

## Prime cause  
Public holidays cause a substantial drop in sales at CBD‑office cafés.  
- **[E4]** shows that for the three CBD‑office stores (CAF‑BRK‑05, CAF‑DOC‑06, CAF‑COL‑04) the sales ratio *sales_holiday_to_normal* is 0.57, 0.57 and 0.60 respectively – i.e. holiday sales are roughly **40‑45 % lower** than on normal days.  
- No other store type (suburban, etc.) shows such a steep holiday dip; their ratios are near 1.0 (e.g. CAF‑HAW‑07 0.99, CAF‑CAM‑08 1.0).  

## Suspects  
| Suspect | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Public holidays** (CBD‑office area) | **[E4]** – holiday‑to‑normal sales ratios 0.57‑0.60 for CAF‑BRK‑05, CAF‑DOC‑06, CAF‑COL‑04 | **Primary explanation** |
| Rainy days | **[E3]** – CBD‑office stores have *sales_rain_to_dry* ≈ 0.96 (only a 4 % drop), well within normal variance | Ruled out as main cause |
| Promotions | **[E5]** – promo days for CAF‑HAW‑07 show virtually identical sales and waste to non‑promo days | Ruled out |
| Weekly waste spikes (E2) | Waste ratios > 2× median, but these are waste‑focused, not sales‑volume; no link to “quiet” sales days | Ruled out |

## Ruled out  
- **Rain effect** – sales only 4 % lower on rain days for CBD‑office stores ([E3]).  
- **Promotional activity** – no measurable sales lift or drop on promo days ([E5]).  
- **General waste anomalies** – high waste ratios ([E2]) reflect preparation or handling issues, not reduced customer demand.  

## Verdict and recommendation  
The unusually quiet days at the CBD‑office cafés are best explained by the impact of public holidays, which cut sales to roughly 60 % of normal levels at the three affected stores.  

**Recommendation:**  
1. **Plan staffing and inventory** around known holiday periods for CBD‑office locations; anticipate lower footfall and adjust ordering accordingly.  
2. **Consider targeted holiday‑specific promotions** (e.g., special menu items or loyalty bonuses) to mitigate the sales dip, since baseline promotions have shown no negative effect ([E5]).  
3. **Monitor daily sales** around holidays using the `daily_detail` tool to fine‑tune future forecasts.  

*No other factor in the evidence provides a comparable explanation for the observed quiet days.*

WITH base AS (
  SELECT s.date AS date,

  FROM cases.sales_table s
  JOIN cases.waste_table x
    ON s.date = x.date AND s.store_id = x.store_id AND s.product = x.product
),
hb AS (
  SELECT b.*, CASE WHEN h.hdate IS NULL THEN 0 ELSE 1 END AS is_holiday
  FROM base b
  LEFT JOIN (SELECT DISTINCT date AS hdate FROM cases.holidays_table) h
    ON b.date = h.hdate
)
SELECT store_id,
       MAX(area_type) AS area_type,
       SUM(is_holiday) AS holiday_rows,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN is_holiday = 1 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_holiday,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN is_holiday = 0 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_normal,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN is_holiday = 1 THEN units_sold END)
             / AVG(CASE WHEN is_holiday = 0 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS sales_holiday_to_normal
FROM hb
GROUP BY store_id
ORDER BY sales_holiday_to_normal ASC
