# CASE FILE
**Question:** Our outdoor‑seating cafés seem to sell less and waste more on certain days. Find out why.

## Prime cause
Rainy days (days with precipitation > 5 mm) cause a significant drop in sales and a sharp rise in waste for all three outdoor‑seating stores.  
- **CAF‑BRU‑03** (suburban, outdoor seating = 1): sales rain‑to‑dry = 0.69, waste pct rain = 56.07 % vs waste pct dry = 8.7 % [E3].  
- **CAF‑FIT‑01** (suburban, outdoor seating = 1): sales rain‑to‑dry = 0.70, waste pct rain = 54.79 % vs waste pct dry = 8.72 % [E3].  
- **CAF‑STK‑02** (suburban, outdoor seating = 1): sales rain‑to‑dry = 0.70, waste pct rain = 55.56 % vs waste pct dry = 8.77 % [E3].  

On rain days these stores sell roughly 30 % fewer units while the waste percentage jumps from the normal ~8–9 % to over 54–56 %, indicating that unsold stock is being discarded because it cannot be sold outdoors when it rains.

## Suspects
- **Rain** – supported by E3; the data show the only systematic drop in sales and spike in waste for outdoor‑seating locations.  
- **Weekly waste spikes** (E2) do occur for some store‑product combos (e.g., CAF‑BRU‑03 Ham & Cheese Croissant ratio 3.29), but these are not confined to rain days and involve indoor‑seating stores as well, so they are secondary factors.

## Ruled out
- **Public holidays** – E4 shows sales holiday‑to‑normal ≈ 1.0 for all suburban outdoor‑seating stores (CAF‑CAM‑08 = 1.0, CAF‑STK‑02 = 1.0, CAF‑FIT‑01 = 1.01, CAF‑BRU‑03 = 1.02), meaning holidays do not depress sales or increase waste.  
- **Promotions** – E5 only reports data for CAF‑HAW‑07 (outdoor seating = 0); no promo effect is demonstrated for the outdoor‑seating cafés.  
- **Other weather or seasonal factors** – No evidence in E1‑E5 links non‑rain weather to the observed sales drop or waste surge for outdoor‑seating sites.

## Verdict and recommendation
**Rain is the prime driver** of reduced sales and elevated waste for outdoor‑seating cafés. On days with > 5 mm precipitation these stores sell roughly one‑third less and waste more than six times the normal rate.

**Recommendation:**  
1. **Adjust production forecasting** for outdoor‑seating locations on rain‑forecasted days – lower baked‑goods quantities to match expected lower foot‑traffic.  
2. **Implement rain‑day inventory controls** (e.g., move surplus stock to indoor seating or offer limited‑time indoor specials) to prevent the large waste‑rate increase observed.  
3. **Monitor daily weather forecasts** and trigger the above controls automatically when precipitation > 5 mm is predicted, thereby reducing both lost sales and unnecessary waste.  

No other factor in the evidence explains the pattern; focusing on rain‑day operational adjustments will address the core issue.

),
rb AS (
  SELECT b.*, CAST(w.precipitation_mm AS DOUBLE) AS rain_mm
  FROM base b
  JOIN cases.weather_table w ON b.date = w.date
)
SELECT store_id,
       MAX(outdoor) AS outdoor_seating,
       MAX(area_type) AS area_type,
       SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) AS rain_rows,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_rain,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS avg_sales_dry,
       ROUND(AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END)
             / AVG(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS sales_rain_to_dry,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_wasted END)
             / SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm > 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS waste_pct_rain,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_wasted END)
             / SUM(CASE WHEN rain_mm <= 5 THEN units_sold END), 2) AS waste_pct_dry
FROM rb
GROUP BY store_id
ORDER BY sales_rain_to_dry ASC
