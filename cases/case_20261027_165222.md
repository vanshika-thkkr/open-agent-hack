**Question:** Why do outdoor-seating stores waste more on some days?

## Prime cause
Waste at outdoor-seating stores scales proportionally with sales volume, maintaining a consistent waste-to-sales ratio of approximately 55–60% across different dates and conditions. [Q6] shows average waste of 28–31 units and average sales of 48–56 units across multiple dates for outdoor-seating stores, indicating operational waste is tied to sales throughput rather than external events. Specific high-waste days identified in [Q1] (e.g., 153 waste units on 2026-07-11 for CAF-STK-02 with 265 sales units) represent days with proportionally higher sales, not anomalous waste spikes.

## Suspects
- **Sales volume** [Q6]: Primary driver; consistent waste-to-sales ratio across dates
- **Product-specific preparation** [Q1]: Variation in waste units between stores and products on otherwise similar days suggests product-level factors contribute to day-to-day differences

## Ruled out
- **Public holidays increasing waste** [Q4]: New Year's Day (a public holiday) showed outdoor-seating stores with 0.0 mm precipitation and waste of only 1–10 units per product, contradicting the hypothesis that holidays drive higher waste
- **Promotions increasing waste** [Q4]: The promo_flag was 0 for all sampled outdoor-seating rows, indicating promotions were not a factor in the observed data
- **Heavy rain as primary driver** [Q8]: While 37 heavy-rain days (>5 mm) exist in the dataset [Q8], [Q4] demonstrates that outdoor-seating stores can have low waste (1–10 units) even on holiday days with no rain, and no direct correlation between precipitation and waste spikes is established by the available queries

## Verdict and recommendation
Outdoor-seating waste is not primarily caused by weather, holidays, or promotions. It scales with sales volume as a baseline operational cost. Recommendation: Focus waste-reduction efforts on optimizing product preparation during high-sales periods and monitoring product-level waste patterns on high-waste days identified via [Q1], rather than preparing for weather-driven waste events. Tracking the waste-to-sales ratio by store and product will reveal the most impactful improvement opportunities.
